Part 2: Partner Selection & Implementation

/* Level 1 — Section 2.1 (Lead partner onboarding and implementation activities from concept through operational launch) */

Responsibility #1
	Lead partner onboarding and implementation activities from concept through operational launch.
At first glance this sounds like:
	"Set up a new airline partner."
But in reality it means:
	Take a transportation solution from business idea to live operation without disrupting the FedEx network.
Many people think selecting the airline is the difficult part.
Actually, the difficult part is making thousands of shipments move successfully on Day 1.
A great partner with poor implementation becomes a network failure.

Scenario 1: What Actually Triggers This Work?
Imagine the Network Planning team has identified a problem.
Current route:
SGN → CAN → MEM
is reaching maximum capacity.
Data shows:
	• Volume growth +25%
	• Peak season approaching
	• Existing airline partner nearly full
	• Service reliability deteriorating
Management decides:
	We need a new capacity solution.
After evaluation, Cathay Cargo is selected.
The selection process is complete.
Now your job begins.
Because no shipment has moved yet.

Step 1: Understand Exactly What Is Being Implemented
Before doing anything, understand the project scope.
Ask:
What lane is being launched?
Example:
SGN → HKG → MEM
or
HAN → ICN → ANC → MEM
Every lane has different operational requirements.

What products will use this partner?
Examples:
	• International Priority (IP)
	• International Economy (IE)
	• Healthcare shipments
	• High-value cargo
	• Dangerous Goods
Different products have different handling requirements.

What capacity is needed?
Example:
Current demand: 55 tons/day
Current network: 40 tons/day
Gap: 15 tons/day
Partner commitment: 20 tons/day
Now you know the business objective.

Step 2: Build the Project Team
This is where many new engineers get surprised.
You are not simply working with an airline.
You are coordinating multiple departments.
Typical stakeholders include:
Network Planning
Provides:
	• demand forecasts
	• route requirements
	• capacity needs

Air Operations
Responsible for:
	• cargo movement
	• sort operations
	• flight coordination

Procurement
Responsible for:
	• commercial negotiations
	• pricing agreements
	• vendor setup

Legal
Responsible for:
	• contracts
	• liability terms
	• risk protection

Finance
Responsible for:
	• budgeting
	• payment setup
	• cost approval

Customs & Regulatory
Responsible for:
	• compliance requirements
	• permits
	• import/export restrictions

IT / Systems Teams
Responsible for:
	• shipment visibility
	• interface setup
	• operational systems

Airline Partner
Responsible for:
	• capacity availability
	• operational execution
	• service performance

As Implementation Lead your role is often:
	Keep all stakeholders moving toward a single launch date.

Step 3: Conduct Project Kick-Off
Now the project officially starts.
Typical meeting agenda:
Business Objective
Add:
20 tons/day capacity
for Vietnam-USA shipments.

Launch Date
Example:
1 October

Scope
Included:
✅ SGN exports
Excluded:
❌ HAN exports
❌ Imports

Roles & Responsibilities
Who owns:
	• contract?
	• systems?
	• training?
	• testing?
	• launch approval?
Without ownership, implementation stalls.

Step 4: Create an Implementation Plan
Good engineers organize complexity.
Example:
Activity	Owner	Deadline
Contract Signature	Procurement	15 Sep
SOP Development	Operations	18 Sep
Systems Setup	IT	22 Sep
User Testing	Project Team	25 Sep
Training	Operations	27 Sep
Go-Live Review	All Teams	29 Sep
Launch	All Teams	1 Oct
This becomes the project's roadmap.

Step 5: Design the Operational Process
This is where theory meets reality.
Questions include:
Where is cargo handed over?
Example:
FedEx truck arrives at:
Cathay Cargo Terminal
Hong Kong.
Who receives the cargo?
Who signs acceptance?
When does responsibility transfer?

What are cut-off times?
Example:
Cargo acceptance: 18:00
Flight departure: 21:00
Can FedEx consistently meet the cut-off?

What happens if cargo misses the flight?
This must be defined before launch.
Possible contingency:
	• Rebook next flight
	• Reroute through ICN
	• Use charter backup
A good implementation anticipates failures.

Step 6: Systems Integration
A solution is useless if systems cannot communicate.
Ask:
Can we track shipments?
Customers expect visibility.
Status updates include:
	• received
	• loaded
	• departed
	• arrived
	• delivered
If tracking disappears, Customer Service becomes blind.

Can systems exchange data?
Need:
	• booking information
	• flight schedules
	• status messages
	• exception notifications
Typically through:
	• EDI
	• Cargo messaging platforms
	• Airline interfaces

Can Finance capture costs correctly?
If invoices cannot be reconciled:
Cost control fails.

Step 7: Build Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)
Every new partner requires documented procedures.
Examples:
Cargo Acceptance SOP
Questions:
	• What documents are required?
	• Who verifies them?
	• What happens if documentation is incomplete?

Disruption SOP
Flight delayed.
What happens next?
Who gets notified?
Who escalates?
Who approves recovery plans?

Dangerous Goods SOP
Questions:
	• Special storage?
	• Additional inspection?
	• Regulatory requirements?
The goal is:
	No improvisation during live operations.

Step 8: Conduct Readiness Reviews
Before launch, perform a final assessment.
Checklist:
Contract Signed?
✅ Yes

Capacity Confirmed?
✅ Yes

Systems Tested?
✅ Yes

Operations Ready?
✅ Yes

Training Completed?
✅ Yes

Contingency Plan Established?
✅ Yes
Only after all boxes are checked should launch proceed.

Step 9: Test the End-to-End Process
Think of this as network rehearsal.
Examples:
Booking Test
Can shipments be booked correctly?

Tracking Test
Do status messages appear?

Recovery Test
Can the process handle disruptions?

Billing Test
Are charges calculated correctly?
Many hidden problems are found here.
That is why testing is critical.

Step 10: Launch (Go-Live)
The partner officially becomes part of the network.
Example:
1 October
First shipment:
SGN → HKG → MEM
Everyone watches carefully.
Typical launch metrics:
KPI	Target
Capacity Utilization	>75%
On-Time Departure	>95%
On-Time Arrival	>95%
Visibility Compliance	100%
Missed Connections	<2%

Step 11: Hypercare Period
Most implementation projects don't end after launch.
They enter hypercare.
Usually the first few weeks.
Monitor:
	• flight delays
	• missed scans
	• tracking failures
	• customs issues
	• billing problems
	• terminal bottlenecks
Example:
Problem:
15% of shipments missing arrival scans.
Root Cause:
Incorrect EDI message mapping.
Action:
IT and airline correct interface settings.
Problem solved.

Step 12: Transition to Business-as-Usual
Once performance stabilizes:
Ownership transfers to operational teams.
The partner is now part of the permanent network.
Success means:
✅ Capacity delivered
✅ Service maintained
✅ Customers unaffected
✅ Costs controlled
✅ No major disruptions

What FedEx Will Actually Expect From You
At Engineer level, FedEx is generally not expecting you to negotiate airline contracts worth millions of dollars.
They want to know whether you can:
✅ Manage a project
✅ Coordinate stakeholders
✅ Identify implementation risks
✅ Build timelines
✅ Track milestones
✅ Support testing
✅ Solve operational issues
✅ Communicate effectively
✅ Drive a launch successfully

How Your Current Experience Translates

CEBS monitoring	Launch performance monitoring
RPI investigations	Post-launch issue resolution
AWB tracking	Shipment visibility validation
Escalation management	Go-live support
Cross-functional coordination	Stakeholder management
Power Query dashboards	Launch reporting

Interview Mindset
For Part 1.1, the mindset was:
	"Given a capacity problem, how do I identify and evaluate the right transportation partner?"
For Part 2.1, the mindset becomes:
	"The partner has already been selected. How do I safely transform that decision into a functioning, revenue-generating operation without breaking service, visibility, compliance, or customer experience?"
That is the real day-to-day meaning of:
	"Lead partner onboarding and implementation activities from concept through operational launch."
And honestly Ben, this responsibility aligns surprisingly well with your CEBS, RPI, AWB investigation, escalation management, stakeholder coordination, and Power Query experience. The job is less about being an airline expert and more about being a structured project leader who can move a solution from idea → implementation → stable operation.
