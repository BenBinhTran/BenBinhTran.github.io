Engineer – Air Network & Partner Management
Federal Express Corporation AMEA (Phoenix Express) · Job ID: RC781658 · Full-time, Professional, Regular (44 hrs/week)
📍 SG & HCMC & Jupiter
📋 Position Summary
The Engineer / Senior Engineer, Partner Network Management is responsible for the strategic planning, development, implementation, and ongoing optimization of third-party air network capacity across Asia Pacific. This role serves as a key liaison between network planning, airline partners, operations, procurement, commercial teams, and external vendors to ensure partner capacity solutions deliver optimal service, reliability, flexibility, and cost performance. Open to multiple seniority levels (0–10 years' experience). Focused on Southeast Asia, though geographic scope can change based on business priorities. Requires: airline network expertise · commercial acumen · operational understanding · strong project management skills.

Key Responsibilities - 1
Partner Network Strategy & Development
📌 Identify, evaluate, and recommend airline, charter, interline, and transportation partners to support evolving network requirements across Asia Pacific.
💡 Real Meaning: Solve capacity and service problems using external transportation partners — not just "find airlines."
🔥 Scenario — What Triggers This Work?
Vietnam discovers route SGN → CAN → MEM is overloaded: • Volume growing 20% • Peak season approaching • Existing airline partner nearly full • Service failures increasing Management asks: "We need more capacity from Vietnam to North America." Your job begins.
Step 1 — Understand the Business Need
Volume Current = 40 tons/day | Forecast = 55 tons/day | Gap = 15 tons/day Service Requirement Priority Overnight? International Priority? Economy? Different products = different transit times. Geography Ho Chi Minh · Hanoi · Bangkok · Jakarta · Kuala Lumpur — all have different airport constraints.
Step 2 — Identify Potential Partners
Commercial Airlines Singapore Airlines Cargo · Cathay Cargo · Korean Air Cargo · China Airlines Cargo · EVA Air Cargo · Vietnam Airlines Cargo · ANA Cargo · Japan Airlines Cargo Charter Providers (temporary surges): Atlas Air · Kalitta · Air Atlanta Ground Transportation: Bonded trucking / linehaul (e.g. Singapore → KL may not need aircraft) Interline Partners: Phoenix Express → Cathay Pacific → American Airlines — one shipment across multiple carriers.
Step 3 — Build an Evaluation Matrix
Engineers score airlines objectively — not by preference.

Steps 4–8 — Evaluate & Assess Risks
Step 4 — Capacity Need = 15T/day. Partner A = 8T/day. Partner B = 20T/day → Partner B solves it. Ask: Peak season? Lunar New Year? Christmas? Black Friday? Phoenix Express hates surprises. Step 5 — Service / Transit Time SGN→HKG→MEM = 36 hrs vs SGN→ICN→MEM = 48 hrs. Cheaper but slower — would customers accept? Reliability metrics: On-time departure · On-time arrival · Missed connections · Damage loss rate Step 6 — Cost Airline A $2.50/kg vs Airline B $2.10/kg. But if reliability drops → late deliveries → higher actual cost. Phoenix Express Engineering constantly balances: Cost vs Service vs Capacity. Step 7 — Operational Fit Phoenix Express arrives 11:30 PM, partner departs 11:45 PM → operationally impossible. Check: cargo terminal capacity · ULD handling · customs cutoff · security requirements. Step 8 — Risks Geopolitical (airspace closures, regional tensions) · Weather (typhoon/monsoon) · Operational (congestion, ground handling)
Step 9 — Recommend Solution
Executive Summary Current Vietnam-USA lane will face a 15-ton/day shortfall in Q4. Recommendation: 1. Secure additional capacity with Cathay Cargo. 2. Reserve charter contingency option. 3. Add trucking backup from SGN to BKK. 4. Monitor utilisation weekly. Expected benefits: Restore service performance · Add 20 tons/day capacity · Reduce peak-season risk

💡 Core Question: How much capacity do we need, where, when, and what backup plans exist if something goes wrong?

🇻🇳 Example — Vietnam Market
Daily volume 2026 = 45 tons/day | Forecast 2027 = 60 tons/day | Growth = +15 tons/day (+33%) Current partner capacity = 55 tons/day | Forecast demand = 60 tons/day Shortfall = 5 tons/day → Capacity strategy needed.
Step 1 — Understand Customer Requirements
Not all shipments are equal. Healthcare may require cold chain, fastest transit, reliable routing. Economy can tolerate slower routes.

Step 2 — Understand Growth Drivers
Manufacturing Growth — Vietnam exports electronics, semiconductors, garments. New factories opened. New Phoenix Express Customers — Samsung, Intel, Foxconn suppliers → volume spikes. E-commerce Growth — Customer expectation: 2–3 day delivery → capacity must increase accordingly.
Step 3 — Create Capacity Plan
Normal Capacity (regular airline agreements) = 50 tons/day Growth Capacity (additional contracted) = +15 tons/day Contingency Capacity (alternate carriers, charter, linehaul trucking) = +10 tons/day Total = 75 tons/day
Step 4 — Build Operational Resilience
Scenario A — Typhoon hits HKG Normal: SGN → HKG. Flights cancelled. Backup: SGN → ICN or SGN → TPE. Plan developed BEFORE the disruption happens. Scenario B — Partner Airline Loses Capacity Current partner: 20T/day → suddenly 10T/day. Can another airline cover the gap? That should already be documented.
Step 5 — Seasonal Strategy
Peak Season: September–December (Black Friday, Christmas, Year-end manufacturing) → Volume surge +40%

Step 7 — Operational Fit
• Will arrival timing support sort operations? • Can Customs clear in time? • Is ground handling available? • Do they accept Dangerous Goods? • Are there ULD compatibility issues? • Can they support peak-season overflow? Example: Airline scores highly but arrives 03:00 AM while Phoenix Express sort closes at 01:00 AM → much less attractive.
Step 8 — Risk Assessment
Cathay — Advantages: Strong service history, high capacity | Risks: Heavy HKG hub dependency, typhoon disruption risk Korean Air — Advantages: Strong reliability | Risks: Limited capacity allocation EVA — Advantages: Balanced cost and service | Risks: Limited expansion flexibility
Step 9 — Build Business Case
Primary Partner: Cathay Cargo Meets 15T/day requirement · Fastest transit · Strong OTP · Supports Q4 peak growth Secondary Option: EVA Cargo Backup capacity · Additional resilience · Competitive pricing Contingency: Pre-arranged charter provider Use during: Peak season · Network disruptions · Weather events
